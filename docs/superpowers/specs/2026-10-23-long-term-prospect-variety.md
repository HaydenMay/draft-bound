# Long-term prospect variety — planning backlog

User steering during v1.2 implementation: the problem is not limited to Andre Johnson's grades. A catalog consisting primarily of good/elite receivers combined with three manual best-skill choices can produce overly generous potential builds. Record this for long-term planning rather than implementing a wholesale redesign now.

Investigate:

- A wider, representative range of receiver profiles and career outcomes, with a coherent original grading rubric and eras/styles covered.
- Full generated-build distributions after actual player choice, not only uniform raw player draws or grade histograms.
- Best-available, specialist, random and varied creation policies; compare Quick and Full modes separately.
- The availability and cost of S-tier potential, rerolls and source-to-attribute assignment rules while retaining meaningful choices.
- How starting ability, potential and attainable college development should relate, without treating all ceilings as inevitable outcomes.

Open: desired catalog composition, source grade rubric, creation constraints and target rates for elite builds. No rarity weighting, account advantage, superstar bias, blanket downgrading or weak-player quota is approved. Keep naming a famous receiver from automatically implying elite potential in every skill.

V1.2 boundary: preserve uniform eligible offers, fix within-offer duplicates, add mode control and assess the versioned progression change. Any small coverage additions already in the draft are not a substitute for this long-term design. Do not claim the pool issue is solved by correcting Andre or merely adding more names.
