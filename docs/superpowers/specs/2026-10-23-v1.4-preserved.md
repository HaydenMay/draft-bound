# Draftbound v1.4 — preserved design backlog

**Planning reference only. Not implementation authorization or a finished implementation specification.** Preserves Part II of the supplied master document. The original source is copied unchanged beside this file. v1.3.1 is visual/legal polish; v1.4 and v1.5 remain separate future releases.

## Design direction to preserve

- Build a distinct WR, make meaningful college/training/transfer choices, earn better opportunities, and reach draft night in a relatively short, replayable run. Avoid both a dominant universal path and repetitive menu work.
- **Current ability and natural potential are different values.** Source-player choices primarily establish potential. Natural potential stays stable; school specialties may offer a higher trainable ceiling without instant gains. Earned ability never falls merely because of a transfer.
- Audit historical source identities using a coherent, defensible game rubric. The document proposes at least one defensible S signature for each historical Hall of Famer, and a broader range of specialists with genuine weaknesses. The illustrative Tre Tucker/MVS examples are not finalized grades. Do not downgrade real-player signatures merely to manipulate fictional-career Hall of Fame rates.
- Address the broader elite-heavy pool and best-skill selection advantage. Evaluate complete generated builds and actual choice policies in Quick and Full, not just uniform raw draws or Andre Johnson in isolation.
- Initial recruit reputation should blend readiness/potential and influence a **limited** set of school offers. Exact blend, catalog/offer sizes, eligibility, fit and randomness need decisions/simulation.
- Transfers should respond to actual performance, current ability, awards and development, not permanently lock an overlooked recruit to their initial stars.
- Expand a meaningful college catalog with stable IDs, prestige, specialty and coaching identity. One primary specialty is a leaning, not a finalized distribution. School assignments are game abstractions.
- Real college/NFL names are desired future options **subject to an explicit owner decision and legal review**. No automatic approval for names, logos, photographs, uniforms or league artwork; none belongs in v1.3.1.

## Provisional numeric targets—retain without implementing

### School specialty trainable ceiling

| Prestige | Proposed specialty ceiling |
|---|---:|
| Five stars | 99 |
| Four stars | 90 |
| Three stars | 85 |
| Two stars | 80 |
| One star | 75 |

These are provisional, not grants of ability or a rule to lower a stronger natural ceiling. Specialty acceleration and its interaction with the development economy remain unset.

### Continuity/development efficiency

| Path | Freshman | Sophomore | Junior | Equal-year sum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A → A → A | 100% | 95% | 90% | 285% |
| A → B → B | 100% | 85% | 80% | 265% |
| A → A → B | 100% | 95% | **75%** | 270% |
| A → B → C | 100% | 85% | 65% | 250% |

The source calls these owner-approved **provisional targets**, still requiring calibration. Preserve 75% for the late-transfer junior season; do not revert it to the rejected 65%. Calculate from school history/year, not hardcoded school names. These are development efficiencies, not attributes.

Clarification needed: the A→B→B prose says junior efficiency “improves,” but its numeric row falls from 85% to 80%. It can mean improvement relative to transferring again (80% versus 65%), not a year-over-year increase. Keep the numbers intact and clarify that interpretation with the owner before formulas are finalized.

Do not assume the equal-year sums are final career growth. The current code already has year multipliers and diminishing returns; the future design must decide whether continuity replaces or multiplies those factors, plus specialty/participation effects, to avoid an accidental double penalty.

## Training and transfer choices still open

- Weekly choices versus persistent adjustable plans versus a hybrid is **unresolved**. Compare pace, agency and click cost in a playable prototype only after authorization.
- Balanced/Focused/Intensive are current systems to audit, not mandatory final modes. Training a strength must consume opportunities that could improve another skill.
- Bloodline can inform decision structure; do not copy its complexity or mechanics wholesale.
- Connect training gains, ceiling slowdowns, specialty and continuity effects to actual weekly broadcasts, with fast advancement preserved.
- Audit existing coaching before redesigning it; retain once-per-type-per-season unless explicitly changed. Do not create parallel duplicate intervention systems.
- A late transfer can be valuable when it unlocks a constrained specialty; staying can be valuable when many skills need development. Neither should be moralized as loyalty or universally optimal.
- Sophomore declaration needs meaningful trade-offs. There is currently no playable pro-development period. Do not invent injuries or negative growth to force early declaration without a separate design decision.

## Professional careers, honors and achievements

- Re-measure current professional outcomes before tuning. The quoted 48.7%/67.1% Quick and 45.6%/83.9% Full HOF rates are historical scripted junior cohorts, not production telemetry or permanent universal rates.
- Target a range of ordinary, productive, star, elite and exceptional careers. Exact induction frequency, career formulas and criteria remain open; source grades and fictional induction are separate systems.
- Audit `domain/eligibility.ts`, stored profile/frame unlocks and current card customization before adding a discoverable achievement screen. Explain available/unlocked/locked goals, requirements, progress and rewards where appropriate.
- Determine what “Legend” currently means before designing a second system: achievement, cosmetic frame/unlock or career distinction. Preserve those conceptual differences. Rewards remain cosmetic rather than permanent gameplay advantages.

## Important implementation constraints for the later design

The current save validator requires current ability not to exceed the stored trait ceiling. The proposed school system permits earned ability above **natural** potential. A future v1.4 specification therefore needs distinct natural potential, available training ceiling and earned ability, plus versioned rules/compatibility; simply changing one existing number would break saves or erase meaning. Do not alter this data model in v1.3.1.

School/team identities need stable IDs and frozen historical display data. Do not use names as keys or rewrite old draft/career identities during a future real-name presentation change.

Before final v1.4 planning, resolve school/offer counts, fit/randomness, specialty count/strength, continuity combination, training cadence/budgets/diminishing returns, source grading/provenance and pool size, HOF targets, early-declaration incentives, real-name permissions and achievement visibility/rewards. Then produce a separate architecture/migration/UI/test/simulation specification with explicit stop criteria.
