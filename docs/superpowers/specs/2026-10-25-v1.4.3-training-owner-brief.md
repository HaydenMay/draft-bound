# Draftbound v1.4.3 — Training & Development Balance

**Status:** Planning brief; **not** an approved numerical specification or authorization to implement.

## Purpose

Address the training and development problems identified during the v1.4.2 receiver-pool work. Keep this work separate from v1.4.2, which focuses on receiver data, team/player rolls, and one-receiver potential selection.

## Established design context

- **Natural Gift:** Grants immediate ability in its chosen attribute.
- **Other inherited receiver traits:** Set **potential ceilings**, not immediate ability.
- **College training:** Determines how much of that potential the prospect realizes.
- **Positive-only growth:** Preserve the existing design decision not to reduce ability through ordinary development.
- **Practice and game experience:** These are distinct development resources; retain that distinction.
- **School specialties:** Influence development opportunities; they should not automatically grant full potential.
- **Training interventions:** Remain a separate existing system, with an intervention type usable at most once per season and a temporary override of the next training plan.

## Problems identified for investigation

1. **Starting ability / OVR may be too low.** Inspect the current starting-rating calculation and how Natural Gift is incorporated.
2. **Freshman growth may be too fast.** Inspect the freshman-year multiplier and total training opportunities before changing values.
3. **Insufficient opportunity cost.** Determine whether maximizing one attribute leaves enough meaningful tradeoffs elsewhere.
4. **Ceilings may not meaningfully constrain progression.** Verify that training cannot exceed inherited potential and that ceiling proximity creates sensible diminishing returns.
5. **Training focus and switching.** Review the previously discussed consistency/specialization behavior (including the proposed 100/95/90 progression); **the exact meaning and implementation of those figures must be confirmed**, not assumed.
6. **School specialty interaction.** Check whether school bonuses compound excessively with training, year multipliers, or interventions.

## Existing factors to inspect (not automatically change)

- Year-related development factors previously discussed: freshman **1.50**, sophomore **1.15**, junior **0.85**.
- Three practice modes and diminishing-return bands.
- Training allocation, game experience, and four-game simulation blocks.
- Natural Gift starting ability, starting OVR, and all nine potential ceilings.
- Any existing continuity or specialization modifier; verify whether the 100/95/90 values are implemented, proposed, or superseded.

## Recommended diagnostic sequence

### Phase 1 — Read-only code audit

Inspect the repository's actual formulas, constants, training budgets, attribute updates, persistence, and UI presentation. Document the complete development path from prospect creation through college declaration. Identify confirmed bugs separately from subjective balance concerns. **Do not edit gameplay code during this phase.**

### Phase 2 — Baseline simulation

Using the production formulas and representative prospects with low, typical, and exceptional ceilings, measure:

- Starting ability and starting OVR distributions.
- Attribute growth by four-game block and by college year.
- End-of-year and declaration OVR distributions.
- Frequency of reaching ceilings, particularly in freshman year.
- Differences between focused, balanced, and frequently switched training strategies.
- Effects of schools and interventions, separately and combined.

Use deterministic seeds, preserve reproducible scripts, and report distributions (not just averages). Distinguish **ability** from **potential** in every report.

### Phase 3 — Propose alternatives for approval

Compare a small number of focused tuning options, such as starting-ability adjustment, growth-rate adjustment, and opportunity-cost adjustment. Show expected tradeoffs and test results. Do **not** change the core design or commit new balance constants without product-owner approval.

### Phase 4 — Implementation (only after approval)

Apply the smallest approved changes, add regression tests, check saved-game compatibility, and rerun baseline simulations. Verify that the Natural Gift, ceilings, college progression, and downstream draft/card calculations still behave correctly.

## Constraints / non-goals

- Do not alter receiver scouting grades to compensate for training bugs.
- Do not reintroduce three-receiver offers or rerolls.
- Do not turn potential into immediate ability except for the Natural Gift.
- Do not add negative training growth or injuries.
- Do not overhaul NFL career simulation, collectible cards, or unrelated UI.
- Do not invent numerical targets or claim the 100/95/90 progression is finalized without checking the prior design and code.

## Deliverables for a future implementation agent

1. Formula and data-flow audit with file references.
2. Reproducible baseline simulations and results.
3. Prioritized issue list distinguishing defects from design choices.
4. Two or three minimally invasive tuning proposals with quantified impacts.
5. Explicit recommendation and **stop for approval** before implementation.

## Definition of done for the planning/audit phase

The team can explain precisely why starting OVR and freshman growth behave as they do, show how training decisions affect realized ability relative to potential, and select numerical adjustments based on measured outcomes rather than guesses. **No production code changes are required or authorized by this brief.**
